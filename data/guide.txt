================================================================================
                          MASTERMIND: THE STRATEGY GUIDE
================================================================================

Welcome to the ultimate guide for Mastermind, the classic game of logic, 
deduction, and code-breaking. This guide covers the rules, the scoring system, 
and advanced strategies to help you break any code in fewer guesses.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. GAME OVERVIEW & OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mastermind is a puzzle game played between two roles:
*   The Codebreaker (You): Tries to guess a secret combination of colored pegs.
*   The Codemaker (The AI/Host): Creates a hidden 4-peg code and provides feedback.

The standard game consists of a secret code made up of 4 positions, using a pool 
of unique colors. Duplicates and blanks may or may not be allowed depending on 
your specific ruleset. You have a limited number of attempts (usually 10 or 12) to
accurately guess the hidden sequence.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE FEEDBACK SYSTEM (THE CLUES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
After every guess you submit, the Codemaker provides evaluation pegs to reveal 
how close your guess is. Understanding these specific symbols is key to winning:

●  [BLACK / SOLID PEG]   = Correct Color & Correct Position
   This means one of your guessed colors is exactly right and sits in the 
   correct spot. (Note: The feedback pegs do NOT tell you *which* position is 
   the correct one; they just tell you that one exists).

○  [WHITE / HOLLOW PEG] = Correct Color but Incorrect Position
   This means you guessed a color that exists somewhere within the secret code, 
   but it is currently sitting in the wrong position.

' ' [EMPTY SPACE]       = Incorrect Color
   This means the guessed color does not exist anywhere in the remaining unmapped 
   slots of the secret code. It is a complete mismatch.

--- EXAMPLES ---
Suppose the Secret Code is:  [ Red ] [ Blue ] [ Green ] [ Yellow ]

*   Your Guess: [ Red ] [ Pink ] [ Orange ] [ Purple ]
    Feedback:   ● ' ' ' '
    (The Red is correct and in the right spot. The others are completely wrong.)

*   Your Guess: [ Blue ] [ Red ] [ Green ] [ Yellow ]
    Feedback:   ● ● ○ ○
    (Green and Yellow are in the right positions [● ●]. Red and Blue are correct 
    colors but swapped into wrong positions [○ ○].)

*   Your Guess: [ Yellow ] [ Green ] [ Blue ] [ Red ]
    Feedback:   ○ ○ ○ ○
    (All four colors are correct, but every single one is in the wrong spot.)
